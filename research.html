Dissertation

Three Essays on the Political Economy of Nuclear Power
UC Irvine, 2021

This dissertation studies why nuclear power has failed to achieve the success envisioned by its proponents, particularly when that success has been achieved at certain times and in certain places yet failed to continue or disseminate globally.

Chapter 1 examines cross-national variation in nuclear power plant construction lead times, finding a positive association between political decentralization and longer construction durations.

Chapter 2 studies the operational reliability of nuclear power plants globally, extending prior work by Davis and Wolfram (2012) to find that the effects of electricity market restructuring on plant reliability vary widely by country.

Chapter 3 focuses on the United States, presenting empirical evidence on regulatory escalation beginning circa 1970 and its relationship to licensing duration and subsequent plant safety outcomes.

The underlying dataset — a novel global database of nuclear power plant technical, economic, political, regulatory, and geographic characteristics — is publicly available at github.com/a-g-benson/Global-NPP-Database.
